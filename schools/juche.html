FILE 16 // EST. 1955 // SEAT: PYONGYANG // STATUS: THE SCHOOL THAT LEFT THE FAMILY

JUCHE

Self-reliance as state religion: Korea's man-centred rewrite of Marxism–Leninism, in which the masses are the masters of history, the Leader is the master of the masses, and the revolution is inherited by bloodline.

SELF-RELIANCE (自主) METHOD: THE MONOLITHIC LINE MARX: CITED, THEN DELETED

SPAWNED FROM

  • Marxism–Leninism — the installed base
  • Korean nationalism & the guerrilla myth — Mt. Paektu as founding scripture

SPAWNED

  • Songun ("military first") — the 1990s extension
  • Kimilsungism–Kimjongilism — the ideology renamed for its owners

SCHISMS

  • vs. Soviet & Chinese tutelage — navigated, never declared
  • vs. Marxism itself — quietly, clause by constitutional clause

01 Origin Vector

Kim Il-sung arrived in 1945 as a thirty-three-year-old guerrilla captain in a Soviet uniform, installed by Moscow in its half of a peninsula partitioned in an afternoon. The apprentice learned the sponsor's lessons — purge rivals early, write the history yourself — and then the Korean War burned the sponsorship into resentment: Soviet caution, Chinese armies on Korean soil, and an armistice signed over his head taught Kim what dependence costs.

The word arrived in December 1955, in a speech scolding party propagandists for copying Soviet models: juche — subjecthood, self-reliance — "the revolution in our country is our revolution." What began as a bureaucratic slap became, through the 1960s, a doctrine; through the 1970s (systematized by ideologue Hwang Jang-yop, who later defected and denounced his own creation), a philosophy; and by the 1972 constitution, the state ideology outright. The genius of the move was diplomatic as much as doctrinal: positioned between quarrelling Moscow and Beijing (Schism №10), Pyongyang used self-reliance to take aid from both and orders from neither.

02 Core Doctrine

Man is the master

The official philosophy: man, not material forces, decides everything — "man is the master of everything and decides everything." Marxism's economics demoted; will, consciousness and organization promoted. Determinism out, voluntarism in.

The three pillars

Chaju (political independence), charip (economic self-sufficiency), chawi (military self-defense). No flunkeyism (sadae — serving the great, the sin of Korea's old elites), no dependence, no imported line.

The Leader theory

The masses are the motor of history, but only when given consciousness by the party, which is given consciousness by the Suryong — the Leader. The "socio-political organism": Leader as brain, party as nerves, masses as body. Cells do not vote on the brain.

The Monolithic Ideological System

The Ten Principles (1974) — memorized by every citizen — establish the Leader's word as the sole interpretive authority, and his family line ("the Mt. Paektu bloodline") as its custodian. The archive notes this solves, by heredity, the succession problem that broke every other file in the building.

Songun

"Military first" (1990s): with the socialist bloc gone and famine at the door, the army becomes the revolution's core institution and first claimant on resources. Juche's survival patch, running since.

04 In Practice

For its first decades the model looked, from a distance, like a contender: postwar reconstruction outpaced the South into the 1960s, heavy industry rose on Soviet and Chinese aid discreetly rebadged as self-reliance, and Pyongyang's delegations toured the Non-Aligned world selling Juche as the decolonized future. The bill arrived with the sponsors' collapse: cut off in 1991, the self-reliant economy proved to have been reliant after all. The 1990s famine — the "Arduous March" — killed hundreds of thousands at minimum; the state answered with Songun, markets tolerated at the margins, and, from 2006, nuclear tests: chawi achieved in its most literal isotope.

Doctrinally, the founders were edited out with characteristic patience: Marxism–Leninism lost its constitutional billing in 1992, the last reference to communism itself was deleted in 2009, and the ideology now answers to "Kimilsungism–Kimjongilism." Three generations — Kim Il-sung, Kim Jong-il, Kim Jong-un — have now held the office the first still occupies eternally. Every other school in this archive files Juche somewhere between apostasy and monarchy; Juche, consistent to the last, does not ask for their recognition.

05 Schisms & Enemies

  • vs. tutelage, both flavors. The August 1956 faction incident: pro-Soviet and pro-Chinese party factions moved against Kim and were purged — the only case on record of Moscow and Beijing jointly intervening to reverse a purge, and failing. Juche's true founding date, by the archive's reckoning.
  • vs. Marxism, by subtraction. No formal schism was ever declared; the classics were simply cited less each decade until the constitution forgot them. The quietest apostasy on file.
  • vs. its own theorist. Hwang Jang-yop, Juche's chief systematizer, defected south in 1997 and spent his remaining years explaining that the humanism he drafted had been rebuilt into a "theology of the Leader." The file retains both versions.

07 Legacy

Juche is the archive's limit case: what happens when the vanguard party's logic — the line above the class, the Leader above the line — is run to completion without external correction. It solved problems that destroyed its siblings (succession, de-Stalinization, bloc collapse) and paid with everything else. As theory it has no serious foreign adherents left; as practice it administers twenty-five million people and a nuclear arsenal, which is more than most files in this building can currently claim. The archive shelves it under both "communism" and "departure from," and lets the reader file the difference.
