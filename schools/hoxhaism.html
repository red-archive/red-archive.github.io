01 Origin Vector

Enver Hoxha — French-educated schoolteacher from Gjirokastër, tobacconist's clerk in occupied Tirana — founded the Albanian Communist Party in 1941 and rode the Partisan war to power in Europe's poorest, most clan-bound country. His formation was double: Stalinism's methods learned faithfully, and small-nation paranoia learned honestly — Albania had spent five centuries being partitioned by whoever was passing. The two lessons fused into a single instinct: every ally is a future occupier with better manners.

History kept confirming it. Belgrade treated postwar Albania as a province-in-waiting until the 1948 Tito–Stalin split let Hoxha purge the pro-Yugoslav wing (executing his rival Koçi Xoxe) and swap patrons. When Khrushchev denounced Stalin in 1956 and courted Belgrade, Hoxha read his own obituary in draft and swapped again — publicly defying Moscow by 1961, taking Beijing's aid and side in the Sino-Soviet split. And when Mao shook Nixon's hand and propounded the Three Worlds Theory, Hoxha completed the set: China too had gone revisionist (Schism №11). Imperialism and the Revolution (1978) formalized the position: Marxism–Leninism as Stalin left it, defended now by exactly one country, population two and a half million.

02 Core Doctrine

Anti-revisionism, total

The Secret Speech was a counter-revolution; Stalin's line — collectivization, vigilance, proletarian dictatorship undiluted — is Marxism–Leninism, and every departure from it, Yugoslav, Soviet or Chinese, is restoration of capitalism in progress. The file's single axiom; everything else is corollary.

Three Worlds heresy

Against Mao's geometry (align with anyone against the stronger superpower), Hoxha ruled both superpowers equally imperialist and all alignment betrayal. Purity of line over balance of power — the doctrine's proudest and costliest theorem.

Self-reliance, concrete

Autarky as theology: no foreign credits (banned by the 1976 constitution), no tourists, no God — Albania declared itself the world's first atheist state in 1967 — and bunkers. One pillbox per four adults, dug into every field and beach: vigilance you can walk on.

The siege as proof

Isolation was not the policy's failure but its verification: a world entirely revisionist is a world that must be bunkered against. The archive notes the logic is airtight and load-bearing in both directions.

04 In Practice

Inside the bunkers, the record splits the archive's columns as starkly as any file here. Credit: a near-feudal country given universal literacy, electrification to the last village (1970), malaria eradicated, life expectancy nearly doubled. Debit: Europe's most complete police state per capita — the Sigurimi's files on a third of the population, labour camps at Spaç and Burrel, execution or prison for a foreign radio habit, religion criminalized outright, and purges that consumed each generation of Hoxha's own lieutenants in turn, ending with Prime Minister Mehmet Shehu, forty years his closest comrade, dead in 1981 — officially suicide, posthumously declared a lifelong foreign agent for several intelligence services simultaneously.

Hoxha died in 1985; the system outlived him by six years and the pyramid-scheme economy that followed it collapsed faster than the bunkers could be demolished — most still stud the landscape, repurposed as barns, bars and canvases. Abroad, the school persists as a small communion of parties (the ICMLPO and cousins) maintaining, with genuine scholarly diligence, the complete anti-revisionist canon — the archive's most immaculate doctrine, at the archive's smallest scale.

05 Schisms & Enemies

  • vs. Titoism (1948). The founding enmity: next-door revisionism with territorial appetite. Anti-Yugoslavism remained Tirana's one constant through every subsequent realignment.
  • vs. Moscow (1956–1961). De-Stalinization read as counter-revolution; Soviet submarines ejected from Vlorë; the only Warsaw Pact member to leave alive (formally, 1968, over Prague — exiting in protest at the invasion of a revisionist by revisionists, a Tirana specialty).
  • vs. Maoism (1972–1978). Nixon's handshake, then Three Worlds: the last patron anathematized — Schism №11. The world's Maoist parties split once more, into Maoist and Hoxhaist communions that still do not concelebrate.
  • Internal, serial. Xoxe (1949), Gega, the navy (1961), the culture (1973), the economists (1975), Shehu (1981): the purge as governance rhythm, each wave proving the vigilance it was justified by.

07 Legacy

Hoxhaism is the archive's purity experiment run to completion: what remains of Marxism–Leninism when every compromise is refused is a doctrine of perfect internal consistency governing a small mountain fortress, alone. Its 170,000 bunkers are the movement's most honest architecture — vigilance rendered in concrete, aimed at everyone, used by no one. The school survives as the standing answer to a question the rest of the archive prefers rhetorical: yes, someone did hold the line all the way down, and this is what it cost.
