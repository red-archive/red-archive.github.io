ANNEX A // DEFINITIONS & MODERN CONTROVERSIES // THE ARCHIVE HAS A LINE — AND SHOWS ITS WORK

THE LINE

What a communist is, what a communist isn't, what the old guard actually wrote about morals, discipline and migration — and how the twenty files upstairs would classify the people wearing their colours today. Everything below is sourced to what they wrote; where the living are concerned, verdicts are attributed to the people making them.

DEFINITIONS ENFORCED ALL QUOTES FROM THE RECORD VIBES: INADMISSIBLE

01 What a Communist Is

The word has a definition, and it is not a mood. Every file upstairs — from Kropotkin's communes to Stalin's plans — passes four tests that no amount of red merchandise substitutes for:

The property test

A communist seeks the abolition of private ownership of the means of production — factories, land, capital — and their transfer to common ownership. Not higher taxes on them. Not better regulation of them. "The theory of the Communists may be summed up in the single sentence: abolition of private property" — FILE 02, page one.

The class test

The primary division of society is class — those who own the productive apparatus and those who sell their labour to it. Every other axis of politics is read through this one, or it is being read through someone else's.

The rupture test

Capitalism is to be replaced, not managed. The schools upstairs disagree violently on the method — ballot, mass strike, vanguard, foco — but none of them mistook the welfare state for the destination. The maximum program is the program.

The internationalism test

The working class has no country; its unit of solidarity is the class, across every border. This is the test the Second International failed in August 1914, and the failure created the word "communist" as a separate identity — see Schism №4.

History even wrote the entrance exam. The Comintern's Twenty-One Conditions (1920) decided who could call themselves a communist party: complete break with reformists and social-patriots, subordination of parliamentary work to the revolution, discipline, and readiness to be illegal when required. Whatever one thinks of the test, its existence is the point — "communist" was a hard boundary drawn in ink and enforced with expulsions, never a self-applied label.

02 What a Communist Isn't

A progressive

Progressivism is an attitude — that things should improve, along whatever axis the era cares about. Communism is a program about who owns the machines. A person can hold every currently progressive cultural position and be a bourgeois liberal by every test above; most, the orthodox files note, are.

A welfare liberal

Redistribution inside capitalism — food programs, healthcare, transfer payments — is, in the communist ledger, capitalism's maintenance department. Bismarck built the first welfare state explicitly to inoculate Germany against socialism. It worked.

A social democrat

The nearest relative and the oldest enemy. Social democracy accepts the market and the constitutional state permanently; communism was founded — 1914, 1919, in those words — as the excommunication of that acceptance. The two have been separate movements, with separate internationals, for over a century. Calling a social democrat a communist is not generosity; it reverses the founding schism of both.

An aesthetic

Red stars, Soviet fonts (the archive pleads guilty), hammer-and-sickle avatars, "comrade" as a register. The symbols are free; the program is a property claim. The files upstairs contain purges, famines and civil wars — they do not contain a single school founded on a look.

03 The Old Guard on Morals & Discipline

Ask what the founders thought of personal liberation politics and the record answers in two voices — both of them theirs.

The puritan voice is real and well documented. In 1920, Clara Zetkin recorded a long conversation in which Lenin tore into the "glass of water" theory — the fashionable notion that sexual life under communism should be as casual as drinking a glass of water — and into the youth movement's preoccupation with sex questions generally:

"This glass-of-water theory is completely un-Marxist and, moreover, anti-social... Dissoluteness in sexual life is bourgeois — a phenomenon of decay. The revolution demands concentration, increase of forces. It cannot tolerate orgiastic conditions." — LENIN, AS RECORDED IN CLARA ZETKIN'S REMINISCENCES OF LENIN (PUB. 1925)

This was not one grumpy afternoon. The Bolshevik ideal of the professional revolutionary was openly ascetic — Lenin's model was Rakhmetov, the fictional militant who slept on nails to harden himself; the party demanded that appetite, comfort and private life be subordinated to the work. Lenin told Zetkin the movement needed "clarity, clarity, and again clarity" in place of what he saw as bourgeois libertinism dressed in red. The old guard judged every personal question with exactly one instrument: does it strengthen the revolution or divert it? Self-expression, as a value in itself, appears nowhere in their ledger.

But the record's other voice is real too, and the archive files both. The same revolution, in its first years, produced the most radical family legislation on earth: civil marriage, divorce on demand, legal abortion (1920, first country in the world), and the removal of sodomy from the criminal code (1922). Alexandra Kollontai — Bolshevik commissar, the era's most famous theorist of the "new morality" — argued love and family would be transformed with property. It was Stalin's state, not the revolution's critics, that reversed all of it: homosexuality recriminalized in 1933–34, abortion banned in 1936, divorce made expensive, the family restored as a "socialist" institution. The puritanism the modern right admires in old communism is, historically, mostly Stalinism's counter-reformation — and the libertine experiments the modern left admires were shut down by the same hand.

What the old guard would say about today's left is a séance, and the archive doesn't hold séances. What they wrote is on file: politics that begins from personal identity and personal liberation, rather than from class and production, would have failed their first sorting question — not because it was immoral, but because it was, in their vocabulary, bourgeois individualism: the politics of the self, which they considered the ruling class's home terrain.

04 The Capture Argument

The strongest version of the complaint that opened this annex is itself a Marxist argument, and the archive can assemble it entirely from its own files. FILE 11 records the revolution's detour into the universities; sixty years on, the detour owns the map. A politics incubated in the professional class acquired that class's instincts: credentialism, moral gatekeeping, the seminar's vocabulary, and a center of gravity in culture rather than in the workplace. Marxist critics — Adolph Reed Jr. most bluntly — have spent decades arguing that race-and-identity-first politics functions as "the left wing of neoliberalism": a framework in which a perfectly diverse ruling class would be a just one, and which therefore asks nothing of property at all. Barbara and John Ehrenreich, coiners of the term "professional-managerial class," made the structural version: the PMC's radicalism expresses its own class interests — management of the poor, administration of virtue — in borrowed proletarian dress.

Debord's file supplies the final mechanism: recuperation — the spectacle's ability to absorb its own opposition and sell it back as style. By that reading, radical politics as content, merchandise and personal brand is not communism's revival but its most complete commodification: the revolution as a subscription tier. The counter-case — that oppression is material, that no mass movement was ever built by purity-testing its recruits, and that yesterday's orthodoxy also called every new tactic bourgeois — is in the record too, and the archive shelves it in the same drawer. What it declines to shelve is the conflation: whatever this formation is, upstairs it would have a different name.

05 Case Files: The Modern Claimants

  • The DSA. (The full register of living claimants, scored test by test, is Annex C.) The classification here requires no polemic — the organization's own charter does it. Democratic Socialists of America was founded in 1982 by Michael Harrington, an explicitly anti-communist democratic socialist, on a strategy of "realignment" inside the Democratic Party; it is a big-tent membership organization, not a party; it belongs to no communist international and claims no communist program. By every definition in this archive — including its own — the DSA is social democracy: the tendency communism formally excommunicated in 1919 (Schisms №4–5). That is a classification, not an insult; social democracy has its own file, its own martyrs and its own record. But when its members or its enemies call the organization "communist," both are wrong in the same direction, and the Twenty-One Conditions would have rejected the application on page one.
  • The streamer left. The best-known left-wing broadcasters — Hasan Piker is the largest — generally self-describe as socialists, not communists, so the false-advertising charge is weaker than advertised; the classification question remains. The orthodox critique, filed nightly by the smaller and angrier corners of the actual communist left, runs: commentary is not organization; an audience is not a class; and dissent delivered through a platform's subscription machinery is Debord's recuperation running at industrial scale — "champagne socialism" with a donation goal. The defense — mass political education reaching people no party paper ever will — is the same argument every school upstairs made for its own media. The archive applies its standing test: FILE 02 asks about your relation to the means of production, not your content schedule. By that test, commentary of any politics is a media business, and a media business is not a workers' movement — whichever flag is in the banner image.

06 The Migration File

The oldest text in this drawer is also the most quoted, and it says exactly what it says. Marx, writing to Meyer and Vogt in April 1870 about Irish immigration into English industry:

"Every industrial and commercial centre in England now possesses a working class divided into two hostile camps, English proletarians and Irish proletarians. The ordinary English worker hates the Irish worker as a competitor who lowers his standard of life... This antagonism is artificially kept alive and intensified by the press, the pulpit, the comic papers — in short, by all the means at the disposal of the ruling classes. This antagonism is the secret of the impotence of the English working class, despite its organisation. It is the secret by which the capitalist class maintains its power. And the latter is quite aware of this."— MARX TO SIGFRID MEYER AND AUGUST VOGT, 9 APRIL 1870

The mechanism was doctrine from the beginning. Engels' Condition of the Working Class in England (1845) documented Irish immigration as the lever pressing English wages toward subsistence; Capital formalized it as the reserve army of labour — capital's standing pool of the unemployed and the newly arrived, held against every wage demand. One of the First International's first practical functions was cross-border strike defense: stopping employers from importing foreign workers, who often had no idea they were strikebreakers, to crush local disputes. And Lenin, in Capitalism and Workers' Immigration (1913), restated the diagnosis in one line: "The bourgeoisie incites the workers of one nation against those of another in the endeavour to disunite them."

So the classical record is unambiguous that migration under capitalism is managed as a weapon against wages and solidarity — and equally unambiguous about the prescribed response, which is where modern borrowers of the quote usually stop reading. Marx's letter continues into instructions: the antagonism is the ruling class's artifact, and the International's "special task" was to smash it — organize the Irish worker, support Irish liberation, unite the camps. Lenin's 1913 article, in the same breath as the diagnosis, called immigration restrictions reactionary and the mingling of workers of all nations progressive. The Comintern ordered its parties to organize immigrant labour; the CPUSA built itself doing exactly that while the AFL's craft unions — the "labor aristocracy" of communist polemic — backed exclusion acts. In the classical file, the enemy is never the migrant worker; it is the employer who prices two desperations against each other, and the union that organizes only one of them.

The modern left's split on this — a class-first minority arguing that open-borders humanitarianism serves capital's labour-supply interests, against a majority holding that any border politics re-runs the division Marx described — is thus a fight over which half of the same letter to finish. The archive's note in the margin: both factions are quoting a man whose conclusion was neither restriction nor celebration, but organization — the one option, it observes, that requires leaving the house.

07 Verdict

The line exists. It was drawn in 1848, inked in 1919, and it runs through property, class, rupture and internationalism — not through lifestyle, vocabulary or merchandise. By the tests in this building, most of what wears red in the modern West files under social democracy or under liberalism's left wing, and the old guard's own writings — on discipline, on bourgeois individualism, on the uses of division — are available upstairs to anyone who wants to check the originals. The archive's standing caution cuts both ways: the same tests that disqualify the modern claimants also convict most of the twentieth century's actual communists of crimes the modern claimants would never countenance. Choosing ancestors is easy. Passing their entrance exam — page one, the property question — has never been.
